# Yogas — a plain-language primer

A concept primer written for Vedic Astrology in our own words, to help explain the yogas a reading may
name. Reference material for the AI writer (retrieval); the program decides which yogas are actually
present from the chart, and this file only explains what each one means. Yogas describe tendencies,
not certainties, and a single yoga is always read in the context of the whole chart.

## What a yoga is

A yoga is a recognised combination of planets, signs or houses that classical texts link to a
particular result. Some raise fortune and status, some bring wealth, some describe the mind or the
body, and a few mark difficulty. Many yogas have conditions that strengthen or cancel them, so their
effect is a matter of degree.

## The Panch Mahapurusha yogas (five "great person" yogas)

These form when Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus or Saturn sits in its own sign or exaltation **and** in
a kendra (the 1st, 4th, 7th or 10th house). Ruchaka (Mars) gives courage, leadership and a strong
physique. Bhadra (Mercury) gives intelligence, eloquence and skill in trade and learning. Hamsa
(Jupiter) gives wisdom, virtue, teaching ability and respect. Malavya (Venus) gives charm, comfort,
artistic taste and a pleasant life. Sasa (Saturn) gives discipline, authority, endurance and the
capacity for hard-won power.

## Yogas of fortune and status (Raja yogas)

A Raja yoga forms when a lord of a kendra (1, 4, 7, 10) and a lord of a trikona (1, 5, 9) are
connected — conjoined, aspecting each other, or exchanging houses. It brings rise in status,
authority and success, and tends to flower in the dasha periods of the planets involved. Neecha
Bhanga Raja Yoga arises when a debilitated planet's weakness is cancelled (for example its
dispositor is strong or in a kendra), turning an apparent weakness into later strength. Vipareeta
Raja Yoga forms when the lords of the difficult houses (6, 8, 12) occupy one another's houses, so
troubles paradoxically become a source of gain.

## Yogas of wealth (Dhana yogas)

Wealth yogas form when the lords of the money houses — mainly the 2nd (savings), 5th and 9th
(fortune) and 11th (gains) — are connected to one another. Lakshmi yoga (a strong 9th lord with
Venus or the ascendant lord well placed) indicates prosperity and grace. Chandra-Mangala yoga (Moon
with Mars) is a classic combination for earning. A reading treats these as a capacity for gain that
effort and timing bring out.

## Yogas from the Moon

Sunapha (a planet other than the Sun in the 2nd from the Moon), Anapha (in the 12th from the Moon)
and Durudhara (both) describe a well-supported, resourceful mind and material ease. Gajakesari yoga
(Jupiter in a kendra from the Moon) gives intelligence, good judgement, respect and lasting
reputation. Kemadruma yoga (no planet in the 2nd or 12th from the Moon, and none with it) suggests a
sense of struggle or isolation — but it is readily cancelled when planets sit in kendras from the
Moon or aspect it, so it is rarely as heavy as it first appears.

## A few more common yogas

Budha-Aditya yoga (Mercury with the Sun) sharpens intelligence and communication. Saraswati yoga (a
strong Mercury, Jupiter and Venus well placed) gives learning, art and eloquence. Parivartana yoga
(two planets exchanging each other's signs) ties the matters of their two houses tightly together,
for better or worse depending on the houses involved.

## How to read a yoga

A yoga is a theme the chart leans toward, strengthened by the dignity and placement of the planets
that form it and timed by their dasha periods. It is read with hope and balance: a benefic yoga
shows where life can flourish, a difficult one shows where care and patience help, and neither is a
fixed fate.
