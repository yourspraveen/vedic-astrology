# The twelve Bhavas (houses) — a plain-language primer

A concept primer written for Vedic Astrology in our own words, to deepen the explanation of the houses a
reading covers. Reference material for the AI writer (retrieval), never used in calculation. Each
house is read through its lord's placement and strength, the planets in it, and the planets that
aspect it.

## How a house is judged

A bhava describes an area of life. Its strength comes from three things read together: where its lord
sits (a lord in a good house and sign lets the matters flourish; in a difficult house they struggle),
which planets occupy it (benefics help, malefics press), and which planets aspect it. Houses also
fall into families — kendras (1, 4, 7, 10) are pillars of action; trikonas (1, 5, 9) are houses of
fortune and dharma; dusthanas (6, 8, 12) are houses of difficulty and growth through challenge;
upachayas (3, 6, 10, 11) improve with time and effort.

## The twelve houses

House 1 (Tanu Bhava) — the self: body, health, vitality, temperament, appearance and the overall
direction of life. The ascendant and its lord set the tone for the whole chart. Significator: the
Sun.

House 2 (Dhana Bhava) — wealth and resources: savings, family, food, speech and the face. It shows
how a person gathers and holds what they have, and the warmth of early family life. Significator:
Jupiter.

House 3 (Sahaja Bhava) — courage and effort: younger siblings, communication, short journeys, skills
of the hands, and the drive to act on one's own initiative. An upachaya that rewards persistence.
Significators: Mars, Mercury.

House 4 (Sukha Bhava) — home and heart: the mother, emotional security, house and land, vehicles,
education and inner peace. A strong 4th gives a settled foundation to stand on. Significators: the
Moon, Mercury.

House 5 (Putra Bhava) — mind and creativity: children, intelligence, learning, romance, and the
fruits of past good deeds (purva punya). It is a trikona, a house of fortune and the creative,
devotional self. Significator: Jupiter.

House 6 (Ari Bhava) — challenges and service: illness, debts, enemies, competition, daily work and
discipline. A dusthana, but also an upachaya: difficulty here is met and overcome through effort,
and it can give a strong competitive edge. Significators: Mars, Saturn.

House 7 (Kalatra Bhava) — partnership: marriage, the spouse, close relationships, business partners
and dealings with the public. A kendra that shows how a person meets others as equals. Significator:
Venus.

House 8 (Ayur Bhava) — depth and transformation: longevity, hidden matters, inheritance, sudden
change, research and the occult. A dusthana whose challenges, well handled, bring resilience and
insight. Significator: Saturn.

House 9 (Dharma Bhava) — fortune and meaning: luck, the father, teachers and gurus, higher learning,
long journeys, faith and ethics. The strongest trikona of fortune. Significators: the Sun, Jupiter.

House 10 (Karma Bhava) — work in the world: career, status, authority, reputation and visible
action. The most prominent kendra; planets here shape public life. Significators: the Sun, Mercury,
Jupiter, Saturn.

House 11 (Labha Bhava) — gains and networks: income, profits, hopes and wishes, friends, elder
siblings and the fulfilment of goals. An upachaya that strengthens with age. Significator: Jupiter.

House 12 (Vyaya Bhava) — release and the beyond: expenses, losses, foreign lands, solitude, sleep,
charity and spiritual liberation (moksha). A dusthana that, turned well, supports generosity, rest
and inner life. Significator: Saturn.

## Reading the houses together

No house stands alone. A strong lord in a difficult house, or a benefic aspect onto a pressed house,
changes the picture. A reading looks for where the chart is supported and where it asks for patience,
and presents each area as a tendency to work with, not a fixed outcome.
