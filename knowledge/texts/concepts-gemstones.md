# Gemstones (Ratna) — a plain-language primer

A concept primer written for Vedic Astrology in our own words, to help explain the gemstone remedies a
reading may mention. This is reference material for the AI writer (retrieval); it is not used in any
calculation. Gemstones are a traditional remedial practice, not a medical, financial or guaranteed
measure, and a stone is best worn only after a qualified astrologer has checked the whole chart.

## How gemstones are used

In Jyotisha a gemstone is thought to strengthen the planet it belongs to. The usual guidance is to
strengthen a planet that is **weak but functionally benefic** for the ascendant (for example the
lord of the 1st, 5th or 9th), and to be cautious about strengthening a natural malefic that rules
difficult houses. A primary stone (ratna) is costly; a less expensive substitute (uparatna) of the
same colour family is often worn instead. Stones are traditionally set so they touch the skin, worn
on the planet's weekday morning after cleansing, and the matter is always one of personal faith and
careful advice rather than a fixed prescription.

## The nine planets and their stones

Sun (Surya) — primary stone ruby (Manik), a clear red stone; substitutes red garnet, red spinel or
sunstone. Metal gold or copper, worn on the ring finger, on a Sunday. Associated with vitality,
confidence, status and the father.

Moon (Chandra) — primary stone natural pearl (Moti); substitutes moonstone or white coral. Metal
silver, worn on the little finger, on a Monday. Associated with the mind, emotions, comfort and the
mother.

Mars (Mangala) — primary stone red coral (Moonga); substitutes carnelian or red jasper. Metal gold
or copper, worn on the ring finger, on a Tuesday. Associated with energy, courage, drive and
siblings.

Mercury (Budha) — primary stone emerald (Panna), a green stone; substitutes green tourmaline,
peridot or jade. Metal gold or silver, worn on the little finger, on a Wednesday. Associated with
intellect, speech, commerce and learning.

Jupiter (Guru) — primary stone yellow sapphire (Pukhraj); substitutes yellow topaz or citrine. Metal
gold, worn on the index finger, on a Thursday. Associated with wisdom, fortune, teachers and
children. Yellow sapphire is widely regarded as the safest, most benefic stone.

Venus (Shukra) — primary stone diamond (Heera); substitutes white sapphire, white zircon or clear
quartz. Metal silver or platinum, worn on the middle or ring finger, on a Friday. Associated with
love, beauty, comfort, art and relationships.

Saturn (Shani) — primary stone blue sapphire (Neelam); substitutes amethyst, blue spinel, lapis
lazuli or iolite. Metal silver or an alloy, worn on the middle finger, on a Saturday. Associated
with discipline, endurance, work and longevity. Blue sapphire is famously fast-acting and is
traditionally tested for a few days before being worn permanently.

Rahu — primary stone hessonite garnet (Gomed), a honey-brown stone; substitutes orange zircon or
amber-coloured agate. Metal silver, worn on the middle finger, on a Saturday. Associated with
ambition, foreign matters, sudden change and the unconventional.

Ketu — primary stone cat's eye (Lehsunia), a chatoyant greenish-grey stone; substitutes tiger's eye
or cat's eye quartz. Metal silver, worn on the middle finger. Associated with detachment,
intuition, past-life tendencies and spirituality.

## A note of caution

The strong stones — blue sapphire, diamond, hessonite and cat's eye — are traditionally introduced
carefully, because they are believed to act quickly. The gentle, widely recommended stones are
yellow sapphire (Jupiter), pearl (Moon) and emerald (Mercury) when those planets are benefic for the
chart. A reading should present a gemstone as a hopeful, optional practice, never as a command or a
cure.
