# Nakshatra attributes and compatibility (yoni, gana, nadi and the matching factors)

Classical Vedic astrology attaches a small set of qualities to a person's birth star (the
nakshatra the Moon occupied at birth) and to the Moon sign (rashi). On their own they are gentle
character notes; together they form the traditional system used to weigh how well two charts suit
each other for marriage. This primer explains what each factor means and where it is used, in plain
words. None of it is a verdict about a person — it describes tendencies and traditional custom.

## Where these factors are used

The best-known use is **marriage matching** (often called *ashtakuta* in the north or *porutham*
in the south). The two birth stars are compared across several factors, each worth a few points,
and the points are added to a total. A higher total is read as an easier natural fit; a lower total
is not a prohibition, but an invitation to look more carefully and to lean on understanding and
effort. Many families also read a single factor on its own — temperament, say — to understand a
person, quite apart from any matching.

## Gana — temperament

Each nakshatra belongs to one of three *ganas*, a broad temperament class:

- **Deva (divine)** — gentle, generous, cooperative, drawn to harmony.
- **Manushya (human)** — balanced, practical, a mix of self-interest and sympathy.
- **Rakshasa (assertive)** — strong-willed, intense, independent, quick to act.

These are tendencies, not grades: every gana has its gifts. In matching, two people of the same
gana are seen as naturally easy together; a Deva–Rakshasa pairing is flagged as needing more
patience, because a gentle style and a forceful style can misread each other until they learn the
other's language.

## Yoni — instinct and affinity, shown as an animal

Each nakshatra is linked to one of fourteen **yoni** animals (horse, elephant, sheep, serpent,
dog, cat, rat, cow, buffalo, tiger, deer, monkey, mongoose, lion). The yoni stands for a person's
instinctive and physical nature. In matching, some animal pairs are read as friendly, some as
neutral, and a few as natural adversaries (for example cat and rat, or cow and tiger), which the
tradition treats as a sign to be mindful of friction.

The yoni is also the root of a common folk observation: a person is often advised to be kind to
their own yoni animal and not to harm it, as a gesture of respect toward one's own nature. So
someone born under a horse-yoni star (such as Ashwini or Shatabhisha) may be told simply "do not
hurt a horse." This is traditional custom and affinity, offered for peace of mind, not a
calculated prediction.

## Nadi — constitution

Each nakshatra falls into one of three **nadis**, which echo the three *doshas* of Ayurveda:

- **Adi (Vata)**, **Madhya (Pitta)**, **Antya (Kapha)**.

Nadi is read as a person's underlying constitution. In marriage matching it carries the most
weight of any single factor, and two people sharing the same nadi are traditionally cautioned to
reflect, since the custom holds that a shared constitution can tax vitality and family life. As
always this is a prompt for care and conversation, never a ruling.

## Varna and vashya — disposition and mutual ease (from the Moon sign)

Two further factors come from the Moon **sign** rather than the star:

- **Varna** is a fourfold note on inner disposition (Brahmin, Kshatriya, Vaishya, Shudra, in the
  sense of reflective, commanding, enterprising, and service-minded leanings — not social rank).
- **Vashya** groups the signs by nature (four-footed, human, water-dwelling, wild, insect-like)
  and asks how readily one draws the other in, a note on mutual ease and influence.

## Tara — the star-to-star cycle

**Tara** is counted from one birth star to the other around the 27 nakshatras; some counts are read
as supportive and some as straining. Unlike yoni or gana, tara is *relational* — it has no fixed
value for a single chart on its own, only between two stars (or between a birth star and the star
of a given day, which is how it also guides the choice of auspicious timing).

## How to hold all this

Treat these factors as a considerate, old language for describing fit and temperament, not as a
scoreboard that decides a life. A modest matching total asks for understanding and effort; it does
not forbid anything. Shared values, kindness, and communication matter more than any single point.
